# Operational playbook

Read this reference when planning or executing a substantive **policy-to-controls-mapper** engagement. Keep the main skill loaded; use this file for mode selection, evidence design, failure analysis, and completion review.

## Operating modes

- Clause decomposition: convert normative text into atomic requirements.
- Control design: map preventive, detective, and corrective mechanisms with owners.
- Assurance mapping: define evidence, sampling, tests, frequency, results, exceptions, and remediation.

## Minimum evidence record

- Authoritative policy version, scope, definitions, normative language, effective date, owner, and hierarchy.
- Requirement ID, actor, behavior, object, condition, timing, jurisdiction or system scope, and ambiguity decisions.
- Control ID, type, implementation, owner, evidence, test method, pass criterion, frequency, result, and exception.

## Decision rules

- Preserve one-to-many and many-to-one relationships; do not force a single control per clause.
- Resolve ambiguous thresholds with the policy owner and record the interpretation.
- Distinguish design adequacy, implementation, operating effectiveness, and evidence freshness.
- A mapping supports assurance work but does not itself prove compliance or certification.

## Common failure modes

- Mapping broad prose directly to a product name with no testable behavior.
- Using policy restatement as a control.
- Controls with no accountable owner or obtainable evidence.
- Silently excluding inherited, manual, or exception-based processes.

## Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Every in-scope normative statement has traceability or a documented gap.
- [ ] Controls have implementation owners and testable outcomes.
- [ ] Evidence and test procedures are feasible and time-bound.
- [ ] Ambiguities, conflicts, exceptions, and unmapped requirements are visible.

If a checklist item is not met, mark it **failed**, **not applicable with rationale**, or **blocked with the missing evidence and owner**. Never silently omit it.
