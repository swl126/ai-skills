# Composition playbook

Read this reference for substantive writing or revision.

## Modes

- Compose: turn a bounded brief and source register into an original draft.
- Restructure: reorder existing material around the actual argument.
- Revise: improve clarity, coherence, transitions, proportionality, and tone without changing supported meaning.
- Audit: identify structural, citation, repetition, placeholder, and completion defects.

## Genre decisions

- Academic: distinguish literature, method, evidence, analysis, limitations, and contribution.
- Technical: optimize for task completion, prerequisites, exact behavior, examples, failure recovery, and verification.
- Policy: state authority, scope, requirements, roles, exceptions, evidence, and enforcement.
- Proposal: connect need, approach, deliverables, evaluation, resources, and risk.
- Executive: lead with the decision, material evidence, alternatives, risk, and next action.

## Evidence rules

- A source identifier proves only that the writer associated a claim with a supplied source.
- Verify bibliographic identity and claim support separately when the consequence warrants it.
- Avoid citation laundering: do not cite a source for a claim it does not establish.
- Mark synthesis and interpretation as such.

## Failure modes

- Writing in source order instead of argument order.
- Producing polished prose before resolving audience or purpose.
- Treating headings as structure when paragraphs do not advance the argument.
- Adding plausible citations or quotations that were not supplied.
- Using a conclusion to introduce new evidence.

## Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Audience, purpose, genre, and outcome are explicit.
- [ ] Every section has a distinct job.
- [ ] Material claims have evidence or a visible qualification.
- [ ] Transitions expose the relationship between sections.
- [ ] No placeholder, unresolved citation, or accidental repetition remains.
- [ ] Final prose satisfies the requested format and boundary.
